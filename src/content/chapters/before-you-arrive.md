A dinner invitation can feel like an interview with extra cutlery. Start with the parts you can control: know where you are going, who you are meeting, and what the invitation asks of you.

## Make the arrangements easy

Respond by the requested date. Check the time, location, dress expectations, and whether the meal is hosted. Ask the organizer about anything the invitation leaves unclear. If you have a dietary need, communicate it in advance so it can be considered before everyone sits down.

Plan your journey with enough margin to avoid rushing. If an unavoidable delay occurs, contact the organizer with a realistic update. Once you arrive, put your phone on silent and give introductions your attention.

## Bring curiosity

Read a little about the organization and think of a question you would actually like answered. A meal gives you space to learn how people work together; you do not have to turn every topic into a pitch.

**Try saying:** “Thank you for inviting me. Is there anything I should know about the dress code or arrangements?”

## A common misstep

Assuming that everyone shares the same expectations creates avoidable uncertainty. Asking a short, considerate question is more useful than guessing.

**Before you go:** confirm the details, communicate your needs, and prepare one introduction and one question. Then let the evening unfold.
