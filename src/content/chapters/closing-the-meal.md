The final impression is often a small one: a thank-you, a warm goodbye, and respect for the people who hosted and served you.

## Read the arrangement

In an explicitly company-hosted recruiting dinner, let the host manage the arrangements they have made. Do not turn the bill into a public contest. At a different professional meal, payment may be shared or handled another way; clarify expectations beforehand if the invitation is unclear.

When you finish eating, put your used utensils together on the plate. Leave the napkin loosely beside it, and follow the group’s lead about leaving. Thank the staff as well as your host.

## Make your thanks specific

A brief follow-up can mention something you appreciated learning. It should sound like you, and it does not need to repeat your entire application or request a favor.

**An example you can adapt:** “Thank you for dinner yesterday. I enjoyed hearing how your team approaches new projects, especially the example about collaborating across disciplines. I appreciate the chance to get to know everyone.”

## A common misstep

Leaving abruptly because the formal meal is over can make the evening feel unfinished. Pause for a proper goodbye. If you must leave early, tell the host discreetly in advance when possible.
