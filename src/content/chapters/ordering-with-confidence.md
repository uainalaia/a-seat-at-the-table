The best order for a professional meal is one you can enjoy while still participating in the conversation. Look for food that is easy for you to manage and pay attention to any price or course suggestions from the host.

## Communicate clearly

If you have a dietary requirement, tell the organizer ahead of time when possible and confirm it discreetly with the server. You do not need to give the table a personal explanation. Ask the server about ingredients or preparation rather than making assumptions about a dish.

Treat staff as professional partners in making the meal go smoothly. A clear request and a sincere thank-you do more than an elaborate attempt to sound sophisticated.

## Choose what works for you

Water and other nonalcoholic drinks are entirely appropriate. You never need to drink alcohol to belong at the table. TCU specifically advises avoiding alcohol at an interview meal.

**Try saying:** “Could you help me find an option without this ingredient?” Or simply: “Water for me, thank you.”

## A common misstep

Choosing something complicated just to impress the group can make eating harder than it needs to be. Conversely, staying silent about an important need does not help your host or server. A calm, specific request is professional.

If a dish arrives incorrectly, address it quietly with the server. Respectful problem-solving is part of good dining etiquette too.
