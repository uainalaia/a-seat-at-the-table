A professional meal gives you a chance to discover people beyond their job titles. Ask open questions, listen to the answer, and share something of your own. Let the conversation breathe instead of planning your next pitch while someone is speaking.

## Give the table room

Include quieter guests without putting them on the spot. Start with approachable topics such as projects, campus experiences, or what someone enjoys about their work. In an unfamiliar recruiting group, avoid turning the dinner into a heated debate or asking intrusive personal questions.

Take manageable bites and finish chewing before answering. Match the meal’s general pace so you can listen and participate comfortably. Keep your phone out of the conversation unless there is a genuine need you have already explained.

## Recover with consideration

Spills, dropped utensils, and forgotten names happen. A brief apology, a practical response, and kindness to the staff are enough. Ask for a replacement utensil rather than using one that has fallen to the floor. Do not turn another person’s small mistake into a public lesson.

**Try asking:** “What surprised you most when you first joined the team?”

**To include someone:** “You mentioned a similar class earlier. What has your experience been like?”

## A common misstep

Trying to demonstrate everything you know can leave little space for other people. Aim for a conversation you are building together, with curiosity and a little patience.
