Your first task at the table is simply to join the people already there. Say your name clearly, acknowledge the group, and listen when others introduce themselves. If you miss a name, it is fine to ask again.

## Let the host guide you

Look for place cards or a seating cue before choosing a chair. Once the group is seated, follow the host’s lead with your napkin and place it on your lap. Keep belongings out of the way of servers and fellow diners.

When food arrives, notice whether everyone has been served. At a hosted meal, let the host signal when to begin. If the host asks you to start while their food is delayed, you can follow that invitation.

## A napkin is there to help

Use it to blot your mouth gently. If you briefly excuse yourself, leave it on your chair in a way that keeps the soiled side contained, following the convention described by TCU. At the end of the meal, leave it loosely to the left of your plate. Customs vary; considerate handling matters more than a perfect fold.

**Try saying:** “Hello, I’m Alex. I’m studying at the University of Arizona. It’s lovely to meet you.”

## A common misstep

Staying absorbed in your phone can make an otherwise warm introduction feel distant. Put the device away and stay present. If you expect an urgent call, briefly explain that before the meal and step away if it arrives.
